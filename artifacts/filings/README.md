# January 2024: unsealed civil records

Being named in a document is not evidence of wrongdoing. Sources are linked for every entry.

Checked October 30, 2026. Case: **Giuffre v. Maxwell**, U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, **1:15-cv-07433-LAP**. The case caption is retained only to identify the record; this index does not reproduce names from the documents.

## What the release is

The January filings are sets of previously sealed civil-case materials made public under a court unsealing process. The December 18 order reports a document-specific review; the January 3 order and filing notice describe the start of rolling filings. See ECF 1315, 1319 and 1320 below. Unsealing changes public access. It does not turn a witness statement, allegation, or reference to a person into a finding of wrongdoing.

## Finding docket entries and attachments

On CourtListener, search for the case caption or **1:15-cv-07433**, and confirm the court is S.D.N.Y. Search the docket for the ECF numbers above and expand each entry to see its attachments. The direct CourtListener PDF links above were fetched successfully. The docket webpage itself returned HTTP 403 during this research pass, so its navigation and attachment availability could not be checked.

A main entry and its attachments are distinct citations: “ECF 1320” identifies the cover filing; “ECF 1320-[attachment number]” identifies an attachment. Copy the actual attachment number and page from the court header after opening it. Do not infer a citation from a PDF filename, social post or search snippet. The nine main PDFs above were read; the hundreds of underlying attachments were not individually verified or indexed here.

ECF 1324 orders five attachments stricken because disclosure of personal identifying information could threaten safety and privacy. ECF 1326 identifies revised filings. Prefer the corrected, redacted record and check subsequent docket orders before sharing an attachment. This index deliberately links only the orders and filing notices, not the stricken attachments.

## Reading a record responsibly

Distinguish a party's allegation, sworn testimony, a lawyer's argument, an indictment and a judicial finding. Quote only what the cited page supports. Preserve redactions and avoid reproducing victims' or accusers' identities, passenger lists, or allegations about uncharged living people. A public docket can still contain sensitive information.
